DiDi CX Quality Analyst — Business Case

May 2026 snapshot · Market = All · Official formulas · 2,460 QA evaluations · 77,266 CSAT surveys · 994,591 contacts

01 Executive Summary

May performance is not a uniform quality result. Process quality on the audit form is above goal; customer-perceived resolution is not. QA Score is 94.14 against a goal of 85 (green, +9.14). CSAT is 79.95% against 85% (red, −5.05). Official Recontact is 5.83% against 5.44% (amber, +0.39). The CSAT miss is the only KPI more than five points off goal. Recontact is close on the headline because Self Help is 67% of contacts at a 1.22% rate; Phone + Live Chat sit at 15.56%.

The channel split inverts the story a global average cannot tell. Live Chat (85.6% of audits) scores QA 96.01 and fails CSAT at 77.55% with Recontact 15.99%. Phone scores QA 83.04 (amber vs 85) and meets CSAT at 86.26%, with Recontact 13.47%. Agents can pass the Live Chat rubric and still leave the customer unsatisfied and coming back. Phone fails checklist attributes the auditor sees (Time management, Complete and correct information) that the customer, on average, does not punish in the survey.

Week-to-week, QA stays green (92.4 → 95.5). CSAT never crosses 85% (range 79.2–81.0). Recontact improves from 6.33% in W18 to 5.26% in W22 and lands under goal in the last week — without a matching CSAT lift (W22 CSAT 79.4%, red). First-contact containment and a 4–5 star experience are not the same mechanism this month.

Single most critical finding for leadership The official mix says quality is fine and recontact is almost on goal. The live operation says otherwise. CSAT is 5.05 points below 85% — a 1-star cliff (16.5% of surveys), not a mass of 3-star indifference — and it is concentrated in Live Chat and in three Contact reason Lv4 (detail) families: order status / delay, cancellation charge or debt, and refund status. On those reasons QA is typically green. Coaching the global 94.14, or celebrating FCR derived from 5.83% (≈94.17%), will not close the gap. The work is policy, tool, and first-contact truthfulness on status and money — plus a Phone QA recovery plan, which is the only audited channel below 85.

Relationship to QA and CSAT. Volume leaders for recontact are the same status / charge / refund names that lead unsatisfied CSAT. Where QA sample exists on those names, QA is green (see section 06). 06 Combined Analysis

The single operational story: status, money, and Chat. Customers contact about where the order is, a cancellation charge they reject, or a refund that has not landed. On those Contact reason Lv4 (detail) names, auditors score the interaction as compliant; customers rate 1★ and come back. Live Chat is where that story has volume. Phone is the inverse subplot (QA weak, CSAT strong) and is the correct place for checklist coaching.

*A differently capitalized “Refund status and conditions” scores QA 76.4 on n=25 (section 03). Combined merge is case-sensitive. Both labels need a taxonomy fix; the CSAT/Recontact miss on refund is unambiguous either way.

Quantified finding. Status + delay reasons alone: 3,189 + 1,800 = 4,989 unsatisfied surveys and 13,014 + 7,680 = 20,694 recontacts. Cancellation charge: 1,951 unsatisfied and 6,599 recontacts. Refund: 1,181 unsatisfied and 2,901 recontacts. Together they are the CSAT and live-recontact program. QA on the matched combined rows is 91–98.

Root-cause hypothesis. Observable pattern only, not confirmed causality. (1) Order-status truth in the agent desktop does not match what the customer sees, so a “correct” contact still leaves the customer without a usable ETA or a corrected ticket — they recontact. (2) Cancellation-charge and refund outcomes are policy/product constraints the agent can explain inside the rubric and still cannot change; VOC “Refund / compensation not received” and “No solution provided” are the customer language for that. (3) Live Chat volume is where that constraint hits CSAT. (4) Phone QA fails are a separate, real skill/process issue (Time management + critical information) that is not the CSAT driver this month.
